# Full-follow-up recovery analysis

November 1, 2026. Review only. The manuscript, supplement, canonical results and source data were not updated. Researcher/ecological exclusions are unchanged; no baseline-trend or result-direction selection was used.

## What changed

All actual post-designation survey years are included. Designation years remain omitted consistently, and only strictly prior observations define Before. Full follow-up supports55 before–after histories and169 post-protection slopes, compared with54 and162 in the first-nine-year analysis. Slopes describe annual change in protected/reference ratios, not absolute population recovery. No projected years were inserted.

The same-history comparison is essential: restoring later years strengthens several kelp/urchin patterns. Expanding the roster to all histories that now satisfy the same three-survey-year minimum separately adds seven histories, including short late Point Dume records; these change the pooled urchin density results. This does not justify selecting or excluding them by direction.

## Baseline support and assumption sensitivity

The55 baseline-supported and114 no-qualifying-baseline slope histories are disjoint and sum to169. The all-history slopes are an overview; the groups are also exported separately. In the no-qualifying-baseline group, kelp rises (q=.025) and purple-urchin biomass declines (q=.029). These are post-protection associations without a qualifying baseline, not evidence that designation caused them. Baseline-supported slope comparisons remain uncertain. Do not select the group with favorable significance as the paper’s main result.

Segmented linear baseline sensitivities are in pooled.csv: Slope_change compares After and Before slopes; Trend_adjusted_change compares observed After values with an extrapolated linear Before trajectory. Neither resolves broad causal attribution. Kelp full-follow-up change falls from0.659 under the flat baseline to0.176 [−0.631,0.983] after baseline-trend adjustment. Baseline limitations remain despite longer follow-up.

HAC lags1,3 and5 are all exported. Kelp and purple-urchin biomass directions persist, with full-roster adjusted q about0.057–0.095. Purple-urchin density is sensitive to these covariance choices: the lag1 estimate is−0.100 log units/year, versus−0.003 at lag3. The leave-one-MPA check recovers the matched-roster red/purple density slopes when Point Dume SMCA is omitted; this diagnoses influence, not a justification for excluding it. Separate-program checks show that kelp growth and purple-urchin biomass decline are clearest in PISCO; the satellite kelp slope interval includes zero. These are conditional uncertainty checks; conversion/size uncertainty and sampling comparability are not resolved by them.

## Checks and interpretation limits

Nine-year and full-period before–after estimates reproduce the earlier researcher-scope runs. Independent Python reconstruction checks every individual effect/HAC covariance, pooled GLS point estimates, interval arithmetic and BH adjustment. Matched histories are identical between windows; baseline-supported and no-baseline groups do not overlap. The487 protected artifacts remain unchanged. See verification.json.

Linear_diagnostics.csv records residual degrees of freedom and maximum Cook distance. Leave_one_MPA.csv reports influence of each MPA on full-period pooled estimates; program_slopes.csv separates survey programs. These are descriptive diagnostics without additional BH claims. Linear slopes are deliberately simple time summaries, not a demonstrated best nonlinear recovery model. Annual sampling and baseline differences still limit interpretation, and histories with only three years have especially weak support for trajectory shape.

```sh
Rscript code/tools/check_recovery_influence.R
python3 code/tools/check_full_followup_recovery.py
python3 code/tools/report_full_followup_recovery.py
```

The analysis shows that longer follow-up can reveal patterns obscured by a nine-year window, but it does not restore every old finding. Membership, follow-up length, baseline support and the precise recovery question all remain consequential. No manuscript changes have been made.
