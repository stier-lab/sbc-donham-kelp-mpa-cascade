## Gate 3. Sheephead non-convergence

**Cause.** The count model is a negative binomial with dispersion written as `inv_sqrt_phi`; as that parameter goes to zero the counts become Poisson and the likelihood is flat in it. During early warm-up a chain can coast a long way along that flat direction (it happened for the LTER dispersion parameter in every frozen chain; LTER has the fewest sheephead units, so the likelihood holds that parameter least tightly). Once there, phi = 1/inv_sqrt_phi^2 is astronomically large and the two lgamma sums in the likelihood cancel catastrophically. The log density becomes rounding noise, every proposal is rejected, the step size adapts to zero and the chain stays frozen wherever it was, with a log posterior 300 to 8,000 units below the working chains. It is a trapped chain, not a second mode. My first hypothesis (starting values too dispersed) was wrong: narrowing the start did not help, and a smaller initial step size only reduced the rate. **Fix.** The likelihood now uses a numerically stable form (`log1p`) and caps phi at 1e8, where the negative binomial equals the Poisson to about 1e-8. The prior on `inv_sqrt_phi` is unchanged and the likelihood is unchanged to 4e-4 log units at the cap (checked against scipy). The R-hat of 77 in the first report was this failure.

A chain is counted as frozen when its adapted step size is below 0.0001 (working chains adapt to about 0.01).

**Reading.** `rw`: at no effect, a small effect and a moderate effect, the 90% intervals cover 91 to 92% of the time and the false-positive rate is 9 to 10%. For large true effects (urchin layout +2.0, lobster layout +1.93) the estimate is pulled toward zero (bias -0.46 and -0.26) and coverage falls to 0.74 and 0.81: the prior on the first four-year increment, Normal(0, 0.6), treats a jump of 1.0 to 1.2 as unlikely. `piecewise`: almost never a false positive (0 to 1%), but it under-covers moderate and large effects (0.65 at -0.65, 0.62 at +2.0, 0.77 at +1.93) because it shrinks every increment toward zero. All coverage failures are in the conservative direction: real effects are underestimated, none is invented, and no interval excluded zero with the wrong sign except 3% of `rw` replicates at the small effect. Power is low for moderate effects: a true 48% decline per decade is detected 33% of the time under `rw` and 3% under `piecewise`. Limits of this test. The simulator is the fitted model, so it shares the model's blind spots: it has no regional trend (the follow-up under gate 6 adds one and the model fails) and it uses the model's own count distribution (so it cannot show the misfit found in gate 8). The urchin-layout replicates were fitted with the first sampler settings; some variance parameter has R-hat above 1.1 in most replicates, while E10 has R-hat below 1.05 in every replicate. About three-quarters of the replicates ran before the likelihood was made numerically stable; the two versions agree to 4e-4 log units away from the Poisson limit. Widening the increment prior is a model change and was not made.

**Confirmed: this is a flaw in the model structure, not in the data.** With no protection effect and a shared trend of 0.8 per decade, `rw` reports an E10 of 0.47 to 0.69 in the direction of the trend and its 90% interval excludes zero in 23 to 60% of replicates (10% is nominal); `piecewise` reports 0.20 to 0.41 and keeps nominal coverage only because its intervals are wide relative to the leak. Why: the response curve g(age) is a drift term that all protected stations share after designation, and reference stations have no matching term. Region-year effects are independent from year to year and shrunk toward zero, and each station's AR(1) deviations are persistent enough to absorb a slow trend station by station. A common trend therefore lands in the AR(1) deviations at reference stations and in g(age) at protected stations. The gate-5 simulations missed this because the simulator had no trend. Remedy to consider (not built: it is a change to model structure): give every station the same capacity for common drift, for example a random-walk year effect shared by all stations with regional deviations, so that a trend present at reference stations is attributed to time. Then rerun the space placebo and this simulation; both should centre on zero before any E10 is shown as a protection effect.

## Not verified

- **The trend leak has no remedy tested.** Until the model gives reference stations a matching common-drift term and the space placebo centres on zero, none of the E10 values can be called a protection effect. Subtracting the placebo from the headline is a rough reading, not an estimate.
- The urchin count-layer misfit (gate 8) has no remedy tested; whether it moves E10 or only its interval is unknown.
- The area actually searched in subsampled PISCO urchin transects is not in the delivered files; gate 7 is a bound, not a correction.
- Gate 9 fits use 2,000 + 2,000 draws; most do not meet the gate-4 thresholds (marked). E10 is stable across them, but their intervals are less exact than the headline fits.
- Simulation replicates use short chains (2 x 500); E10 converged in every replicate, variance parameters often did not.
- The space placebo's relabellings share most of their data, so the count of intervals excluding zero has no clean reference distribution. The verdict rule for it was written after seeing the results; it is stricter than the rule first coded.
- The trend-leak simulation used one trend size (0.8 per decade) and 40 replicates per cell.
- The predictive checks hold latent station-year densities at their posterior draws, so they test the count layer only.
- The 1996 change in the KFM fish count layout is inferred from unit areas in the data, not checked against protocol documents.
- The seven regions in `data.py` are still a proposal. No region definition, station or year was changed, and no site was dropped from any reported fit.
- No stage 2 (multi-taxon), biomass or kelp work was started.
