# Recommended inclusion framework after the November 1 audits

Recommendation for review; not a new frozen primary specification, manuscript update, or claim that every upstream exclusion has been resolved. Based on satellite_primary_20261101/CRITERIA.md, diver_exclusion_refit_20261101/consistent_kfm/PLAN.md, upstream_sites_20261101/README.md and anacapa_comparability_20261101/README.md.

1. Define the treatment biologically: documented fishing protection for at least one focal predator, with no-take and partial protection reported separately. Record sheephead and lobster rules separately; inclusion of an area does not imply both species are protected. Retain the revised 18 satellite candidates; two other delivered labels fail this treatment scope. This is not a census of all possible MPAs.
2. Make decisions for each MPA/program/response history. A missing lobster size pool does not exclude sheephead or kelp at that MPA; it excludes unsupported lobster biomass. Keep biomass and density distinct. Alternative kelp-density and roving-fish records are not extra independent replicates of existing outcomes.
3. Require verified measurements, correct dates/units/methods, documented protected/reference assignments and actual paired calendar years. Correct recoverable problems consistently across old and new histories. Preserve observed zeros; do not turn missing surveys into zeros, interpolate outcomes, or invent size estimates. Truly unresolved identity/reference assignments remain pending rather than being assigned convenient new controls.
4. Separate two eligibility paths. Post-protection association: >=3 paired surveys in years1–9. Before-after change: additionally >=3 strictly prior paired years and supported baseline design. Exclude designation year from both periods. Sparse baseline is not a reason to discard valid post-only observations. Existing PISCO fixed-station branch checks govern its baseline admission; the other programs' retained source assignments are not newly certified as perfectly fixed station designs. Audit unresolved station continuity before claiming a universal matched-station design.
5. Primary window is the common first nine complete post-designation years, using observed years only. Longer follow-up remains a distinct sensitivity; nine years is a comparability choice, not a biological recovery deadline. Three prior years primary/four-year sensitivity remains Adrian's decision; a full common-estimand four-year rerun must be checked before reporting that sensitivity as completed for this expanded cohort.
6. Do not exclude on baseline trend significance, response sign, effect magnitude, influence, or disagreement between programs. Flat baseline primary and trend baseline sensitivity on the same observations. This is a transparent revision after examining data, not preregistration or an outcome-blind audit.
7. Treat habitat/program differences as design information. Retain both Anacapa comparisons and program-specific displays; no post hoc depth band or preferred program. Gull KFM stays primary per Adrian's explicit decision with its contested reference disclosed and omission sensitivity retained. A demonstrated invalid reference would require exclusion; a plausible but incompletely documented mismatch is not proof of invalidity. Apply uncertainty labels consistently to retained and newly added comparisons.
8. Do not count overlapping histories as independent MPAs. Account for shared reference/MPA dependence; show satellite and diver kelp estimates separately alongside any broad synthesis. Their measurement compartments differ. Constant histories lacking sampling variance remain descriptive and cannot receive arbitrary inverse-variance weights. Withhold unsupported pooled precision when independent information is inadequate.

## Consequences of the closest completed implementation

The latest expanded review contains206 candidate response histories,62 before-after fits and191 post-protection fits, not206 independent reserves. The upstream reconstruction adds25 post-only fits to the preceding166; it changes none of the62 before-after fits. Earlier recovery of harmonized historical omissions and uniform KFM protocol repair are separate changes, not all attributable to these25 additions.

Primary all-program kelp change is0.328 (95% CR2 interval−0.129,0.785); trend-baseline sensitivity−0.382 (−1.652,0.887). Post-protection kelp difference is0.152 (−0.383,0.687), versus0.285 before the25new histories. Diver-only and satellite-only primary post intervals both span zero. Longer-follow-up kelp change is0.627 (0.156,1.098), but adjusted p=.114: duration matters, and this should not be hidden or substituted for the common-window primary.

Sheephead post biomass1.209 (0.616,1.803), adjusted p=.006; density0.655 (0.247,1.062), adjusted p=.018. Both remain positive across tested covariance-lag/full-follow-up/Gull-omission scenarios. These are post-protection associations; before-after sheephead evidence is weaker, with biomass lacking enough independent clusters for robust inference. Lobster and urchin primary post differences remain uncertain. Red-urchin before-after biomass has a positive nominal interval, but few independent sites and adjusted p=.305; do not call it a robust cascade result.

Take-home supported by these checks: protected sites support more sheephead on average, but the current before-after comparisons do not establish a consistent regional predator–urchin–kelp cascade or a clear kelp increase attributable to protection. Habitat differences and reference choice affect observed contrasts; uncertainty is not evidence that trophic effects never occur.

The current analysis does not establish the consequences for the paper's resilience, pathway or other downstream models. Those must be reconciled/rerun when a revised primary cohort is adopted. Blue Cavern/Long Point response restrictions and several unresolved reference histories remain outside the completed upstream reconstruction; retain explicit pending rows, not undocumented permanent exclusions.
